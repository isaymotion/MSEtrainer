About MSE Trainer

MSE Trainer helps psychiatry residents recognize mental status exam findings and the interviewing techniques in Shea, Psychiatric Interviewing: The Art of Understanding, 3rd ed. (2016).

Tagalog cases are original teaching cases written for Filipino clinical practice. Their terms still point to the matching Shea chapters (mostly Ch. 16, The Mental Status, and Ch. 20, Culturally Adaptive Interviewing). The patient's English translation appears after you answer. Tagalog audio is the recorded Filipino voice from Usap Tayo, played when you are online.

Suicide and violence assessment, motivational interviewing, and the Medication Interest Model follow Shea's Ch. 17, 18, 22, and 23. Suicide and violence vignettes stay at the clinical level a resident needs and never describe methods in detail. Facilic schematics are drawn with the symbols of Appendix I, and The written document drills the EHR guidance of Appendix III. A term is mastered once you get it right on two different cases.

Culture or pathology? covers Filipino look-alikes such as hiya and blunted affect, a shared belief in kulam and a delusion, or grief and hallucinations. These are teaching generalizations: always ask the patient and family what an experience means to them.

A study aid only, not a substitute for the book, supervision, or clinical judgment. All cases are fictional. Progress is saved in this browser; use Export backup at the bottom of the page (or Back up or restore in the menu) to keep a copy.
